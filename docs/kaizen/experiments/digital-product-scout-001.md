# KAIZEN Digital Product Scout - Experiment 001

**Run date:** 2026-09-20  
**Mode:** live market reconnaissance  
**Status:** WATCH / VALIDATE NICHE BEFORE BUILD  
**Purpose:** Test whether the KAIZEN research method can discover an opportunity from market evidence without beginning with a user-selected product.

## Search frame

The first pass intentionally sampled three common digital-product families:
1. digital planners;
2. Notion templates / operating systems;
3. spreadsheet-based planners and trackers.

The goal was not to pick the largest category. The goal was to identify a demonstrated-demand category plus a recurring unresolved customer problem that could support a materially improved original product.

## What surfaced

### 1. Digital planners show demonstrated transactional demand

Public Etsy pages surfaced sellers with substantial sales/review histories:
- DigitalPlannerStart: about 43.4K shop sales and 4.8 average rating from about 4.4K reviews; current 2026 planner listed around $24.97.
- BrighterPlans: about 88.5K shop sales and roughly 6.2K reviews on the surfaced review page.
- GoodHabitsPlanner: 2,146 shop sales and 196 reviews on the surfaced review page.

This is category-level demand evidence, not proof that any new planner will sell.

### 2. Ease and low friction repeatedly appear in positive planner feedback

Surfaced Etsy feedback repeatedly praises:
- easy use;
- simple/clean layouts;
- flexibility;
- calendar integration;
- beginner friendliness.

There are also friction signals around downloads, file expectations, compatibility and links.

### 3. Generic Notion systems show a stronger pain pattern than a stronger investment case

Recent public discussions repeatedly describe a common failure mode:
- users download elaborate second brains / life OS / dashboards;
- setup creates excitement;
- ongoing maintenance becomes work;
- systems become too deep, cluttered or complex;
- users abandon them;
- simpler systems survive.

A July 2026 Notion discussion specifically describes people abandoning complex templates because maintaining them becomes work. A template critique from the same period warns that a system becomes messy as tasks/projects scale and questions daily ease after setup.

### 4. Niche specificity may matter more than generic template volume

Recent seller discussions claim niche templates outperform generic life planners, and one discussion argues that highly specific workflows are more defensible now that generic templates are easy to generate with AI. These are anecdotal seller reports, not audited sales data, so KAIZEN treats them as hypothesis evidence rather than proof.

## Candidate opportunity thesis

### Working concept: "Zero-Maintenance Operating System"

Not a giant second-brain template.

The product hypothesis is a **minimal, role-specific operating system that minimizes data entry and maintenance**, built around:
- one fast capture surface;
- automatic or assisted classification;
- only the next relevant action surfaced;
- optional voice capture;
- calendar/reminder handoff;
- a very small number of views;
- guided setup;
- modular additions instead of an all-in-one dashboard.

The underlying gap is not "people need another planner."

It is:

> People want the benefits of a structured personal/work operating system without having to operate the operating system.

## Why this is only WATCH

The current evidence is enough to identify a recurring problem, but not enough to invest.

Before TEST status, KAIZEN should prove:
1. a specific buyer segment experiences the maintenance problem frequently;
2. that segment already spends money or substantial time solving it;
3. the pain is costly enough to motivate purchase;
4. existing products do not already solve it adequately;
5. a low-maintenance implementation is technically feasible in the selected delivery mechanism;
6. there is a reachable distribution channel.

## Next research question

Do **not** research "the best Notion template."

Research:

> Which specific professional, business-owner, creator, ministry, logistics, sales, or personal-workflow segment has a recurring information/task-management problem, currently uses templates/spreadsheets/Notion/manual systems, complains about upkeep or fragmentation, and has observable willingness to pay for a simpler system?

This changes the product from a generic template into a vertical workflow product.

## Candidate verticals for validation, not assumptions

- solo service businesses;
- small-business owners;
- sales professionals;
- creators managing content production;
- ministry/church administration;
- independent logistics/dispatch operators;
- freelancers managing multiple clients.

KAIZEN should allow the evidence to select the vertical.

## Experiment 001 result

**Research engine result:** useful signal discovered.

**Product decision:** WATCH.

**Learning:** The strongest initial opportunity is not another feature-heavy digital planner. It is the recurring gap between **powerful systems and systems people will actually maintain**. The next pass should search for the vertical where that gap has the clearest economic value.

## Evidence-quality note

Marketplace sales shown above are shop-level public counters and should not be attributed to a single product unless the marketplace explicitly provides item-level sales. Reddit seller claims are anecdotal and require independent validation. This experiment deliberately records those limitations rather than converting weak signals into false certainty.
